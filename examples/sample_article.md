# A Short Guide to Home Composting

Composting turns kitchen and garden waste into rich soil. It needs very little equipment, and most of the work is done by tiny organisms that are already around you.

# What goes in the bin

A healthy compost heap needs two kinds of material. Green materials are wet and rich in nitrogen. Brown materials are dry and rich in carbon. Aim for roughly one part green to two parts brown.

- Green: vegetable peelings, coffee grounds, fresh grass clippings.
- Brown: dry leaves, cardboard, straw, torn paper.
- Keep out: meat, dairy, oils, and anything treated with chemicals.

# Keeping it healthy

Turn the heap every week or two so that air reaches the middle. The pile should feel like a wrung-out sponge. If it is too wet, add more brown material. If it is too dry, sprinkle it with water.

> Patience is the secret ingredient. Most piles need between two and six months.

# Using the finished compost

Finished compost is dark, crumbly, and smells like a forest floor. Spread a thin layer over flower beds, or mix a handful into the soil when planting vegetables. Your garden will thank you.
